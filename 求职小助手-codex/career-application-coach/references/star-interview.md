# STAR Reconstruction and Interview Map

Use for every complete application case and any rewrite that needs deeper evidence.

## Select cases before writing

Choose two or three cases by target relevance, personal ownership, evidence quality, rarity, and recency. Do not rank a large-company internship first merely for prestige.

## Build a known-fact STAR

For each case, record:

- `Situation`: business context, audience, problem, constraint, or project stage already in the source;
- `Task`: the user's role, objective, and responsibility boundary;
- `Action`: concrete decisions, comparison or trade-off, collaborators, delivery objects, mechanism, and cadence;
- `Result`: metric, qualitative outcome, scope, feedback, efficiency change, or risk reduction.

Add a `case thesis`: what the case proves for the JD, what it cannot prove, and which source detail makes it credible. Missing STAR elements remain `needs confirmation`.

## 经历追问，提炼精华

Provide at least 12 questions: four project deep-dives, four role-professional questions, and four behavioural/career questions. For each question state:

`question -> why this JD asks it -> linked case or JD requirement -> evidence already available -> evidence to prepare`

Examples of useful deepening prompts:

- What was the hardest decision or trade-off, and what alternative did you reject?
- What specific output did you deliver, who used it, and what changed because of it?
- How did you judge content, creator, user, or channel fit before execution?
- What would you repeat in a different project, and what would you change?

## Answer only from evidence

When the user asks for answers, use: `conclusion first -> two or three evidence-backed points -> closing lesson`. Mark unknown facts rather than supplying a polished fictional answer.
